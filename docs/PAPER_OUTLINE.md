# PART II — THE MORPHS: BLOOM · CONVERGENCE · BALANCE (141–496 s)

**The three morph sections are the title of the piece.**

**Narrative sketch.** A material chosen for INT2 — "a harmonically
morphing crescendo" — outgrew its container and became the piece's
center and its name. The machinery was measurement-first (the
quarter-tone patch proved non-uniform; pitch bend measured and chosen);
the decisive reframe was hearing the object as BEATING rather than
pitch sweeping. Three morphs were played in by the composer as actuals,
placed, extended from eight to ten parts, and notated with a
purpose-built vocabulary of displacement-map curves. The performance
notes restate the player's task in six words: a glissando in beating
speed.

## Topics — pick from these

- **Origin: a container material becomes the piece's argument** — INT2's
  menu choice → the center and the title
- **Standing up the morph machinery in one day** (PLAN 2v) — probe
  gates; the quarter-tone patch is NOT uniform (+23 ¢ at F2 → +57 ¢ at
  C4); pitch bend measured ±1.99 st, linear, re-keys with no seam;
  "volume rides every model"
- **The reframe: beating, not sweeping** — the phase-experiment arc;
  E4's clean negative closed the timing route; E6 pitch beating worked
  — the first fully predictive result of the process
- **The morph model + the ear-driven debugging arc** — carrier / span /
  duration / release; cycling; the ending law; the release/cycling
  conflation caught by the composer's ear; the fade-in attack = two
  real bugs; the CC7-timing blip
- **Bespoke shapes over presets** — the 2z engine kept, its presets
  rejected; shapes built one morph at a time (SHAPE_LESSONS)
- **Played takes as source** — model ↔ actual (2y); the composer's
  actuals (ACT-BLOOM-01 "JYBloom001") as the placed objects; siblings
  regenerated and chosen by ear
- **The morph-section form** — a bed with played impacts punched
  through; impacts borrow WHOLE PAIRS, each silencing a beating rate;
  the 15-minute ceiling as a formal constraint
- **Ten parts, five mirrored pairs** — the re-render; pairs are
  consecutive and mirrored; the beating data (2.06 → 6.55 Hz; max
  spread 40.9 ¢)
- **Notating the morphs** — the vocabulary (header pitches · go lines
  per breath · no onset heads · two normalised filled curves · two
  meters); the template only fits BLOOM (CONVERGE oscillates, the tool
  refuses; BALANCE has no glissando at all) — a composer decision per
  section, not a universal template
- **The governing principle of the two curves** — curves are
  DISPLACEMENT MAPS, normalised to their own extremes; "a 20-cent
  glissando over 108 seconds and a two-octave glissando over one second
  are the same articulation" — the composer's own Kobayashi citation
- **The performance notes** — "a glissando in beating speed"; "pitch
  variation is simply the catalyst" (the notated object is an
  interference pattern, not a pitch); the three perceptual regimes
  (countable → flutter → roughness); the demo recordings as part of
  the apparatus
- **Open: the beating indicator in the parts** — committed, undesigned;
  the composer's own doubt on whether the pairs can hear each other

# PART III — THE TRANCE SECTION (500–751 s)

**Narrative sketch.** Conceived by dictation as a Ghost-Trance-like
pulsed field: a continuous pulse ground, pitch material ranging from
single pitches to tone rows, cross-cut — interruption, not transition —
by multitempo and phase-shift bursts. Its machinery predated it, but
this section is where the working method flipped: panels were abandoned
for prompts and console scripts, and the generator was specified by
talking, existing as shared understanding before existing as software.
Form was performed by hand (the live rig's stopwatch log became the
section durations verbatim), assembled by pitch reservoirs and variant
families, and the section served as the notation architecture's first
end-to-end testbed. Notated on day 35 as one long accelerando.

## Topics — pick from these

- **Conception by dictation** — the pulsed field; pitch density as a
  dial (single → sets → rows); cross-cutting as the operative word
- **The machinery it inherited** — phase-shift research (drift per
  attack; the beating model; why two voices always gallop; smoothness
  is spread); the pulse sequencer strip; the multitempo rig; the
  phase-shift selector that needed zero code
- **The method decision** — "panels… aren't lending the results";
  prompts + console scripts; the bespoke frame need not be software
- **The generator specified by talking** — units, species, "the
  accordion thing"; the minimum-rest constraint that beat "rotation
  period" (the plain-language problem statement contained the better
  algorithm); "the documentation IS the implementation"
- **Auditioning into form** — the character × speed confound found and
  fixed; ladders as gestures; the inverted-U of onset complexity
  (machine proposes an order, the ear corrects it); the composer
  composing WITH the discursive quality instead of smoothing it
- **The live rig: hand-performance as formal data** — arrow-key
  performance captured; the stopwatch log rendered back as the score's
  section durations
- **The assembly method** — pitch reservoirs; dictated preference
  becomes grammar ("one long then one or two short"); variant families
  over parameters (compare hearings, not settings); the score as plan;
  the natural-pace ending; endurance, not speed, as the real performer
  question
- **A generator choice silently decides notatability** — fixed-tempo
  assignment gives each player ONE pulse; choose free assignment and no
  metric notation is possible at all
- **The notation architecture's testbed** — the trance section was
  Phase B: extract → coordinates → glyphs → layout → first pixels →
  splice; the study score's beating lane
- **TRANCE A4, the notated section** — one long accelerando ♩ = 75 →
  120 giving the 8 tempo bar lines; 3109 quarter notes, zero flag
  glyphs; 47 end crescendos as surges; all text stripped (markers live
  in the score, not the page); the two seams pruned by the composer's
  rule
- **Scale** — at 251 s the longest single section; the 15-minute
  ceiling arithmetic

1. **The record falls to a frame (the hook).** Coney Island 2001:
   Kobayashi's debut roughly doubles the world record. He hadn't
   out-eaten the field inside the received method; he decomposed the
   problem — bun and sausage as one object, eaten as served — and

   aside.)*
2. **The claim, transposed.** A piece can be approached almost sui
   generis: think about THIS piece's materials with a bespoke lens —
   one that may well include traditional thinking — then build the
   tools that make the lens workable. Explicitly NOT "inventing the
   art of composition": the craft stands; the frame around each
   material is what gets rebuilt. *(Day-14 dictation verbatim
   available.)*
3. **Why now: the cost collapse.** A custom apparatus for one esoteric
   material used to cost more than the material was worth; that cost
   has collapsed. AI is named here, once, precisely: the reason the
   method is affordable, not the subject. *(Forward-pointer to 6.)*
4. **The frame in one image: Gehry/CATIA.** Day-1 dictation, before
   any material existed: the composer owns the freehand meta-shape; an
   engine underneath works out the interior that realizes it. *"I'm
   essentially doing Xenakis, but with animated notation that might
   have more precision"* — plants the Xenakis lineage Part I inherits,
   and the drawn-architecture / engineered-interior split the whole
   piece keeps testing. Confirmed day 36 as "the correct framing."
5. **The method, in six passes.** Conceive sui generis → sandbox →
   explore/refine/document → fix gestures → assemble sonic shapes →
   a SEPARATE notation pass. One sentence each; the parts carry the
   demonstrations. Deferred notation as positive method, not delay:
   nineteen days composed before a note of notation; notation later
   becomes an empirical question about the material.
6. **How AI is treated in this paper.** Not the headline. Two
   commitments: show what it enabled (the fluidity that makes the
   reframe practical rather than theoretical) · be transparent about
   where it was used (tool building, take analysis, generation under
   composer-specified rules, documentation) and where every musical
   decision was made — the composer's ear, at every juncture.
7. **The piece, and the road ahead.** Bloom — Convergence — Balance,
   for tuba ensemble, three parts, ~12.5 minutes. One sentence per
   part, each as a demonstration of the frame: I — the algorithmic
   shape kept, the grain source replaced with live performance ·
   II — a bespoke material outgrows its container and becomes the
   piece's center and its title · III — a generator that existed as
   shared understanding before existing as software. Then the paper's
   shape: three parts, then the close.

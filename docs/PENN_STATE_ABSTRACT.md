# DRAFT B — THE COMPOSER'S OWN, day 37 (2026-08-27) — **VERBATIM, 204 words**

> *Typed by the composer in full. Preserved exactly as written — including
> capitalisation and punctuation — per the repo's verbatim rule. Copy-edits are
> listed below the text and have NOT been applied.*

In 2001 Takeru Kobayashi revolutionized competitive eating. He didn't achieve
this by eating vast amounts of food or by learning how to starve himself so he
could eat a massive amount in one setting. Kobayashi was an engineer, so he
reframed the problem: how to get heterogeneous food material down a narrow tube
into a bag at the bottom. How would mister Kobayashi, break down composition?
perhaps something like this: create compelling sounds, lay them out through
time in a satisfying way. then inspire and empower performers to realize these
sounds. Using bespoke AI tools, I was able to get to the heart of what I think
makes an interesting sound. I was able to pursue many different sound
generation techniques. and audition resultant sound to see which techniques
were creating the sounds I imagined or leading me down an even better
direction. I could quickly lay sounds out in various forms and hear which
shapes suited the materials. I was then able to adapt my research in animated
musical notation directly to the sounds and give performers the information
they need to realize these specific sounds. This is an ear-led compositional
process: using bespoke tools to go where the ear wants to go.

## DRAFT A — the AI's version, superseded by B; kept as a parts bin
## (**277 words** measured; title adds 9)

**Composition by Kobayashi: bespoke frames for sui generis materials**

In 2001 Takeru Kobayashi doubled the competitive-eating record — not by eating
faster, but by refusing the received unit of the problem. The record fell to a
frame.

This paper makes the same move for composition and demonstrates it with a
completed work: *Bloom — Convergence — Balance* for tuba ensemble (ten parts,
12.5 minutes). Each material was approached almost sui generis — define what it
is and which of its parameters matter, build a small bespoke apparatus that
makes it audible and adjustable, audition until the choices are made by ear, fix
concrete gestures, assemble them as sonic shapes. Notation is a separate,
deferred pass: nineteen days of composing preceded the first notated pitch.

What makes this practical rather than theoretical is a cost collapse. A custom
sandbox for one esoteric material now takes an afternoon rather than a season.
AI-assisted tool building is the enabling condition, not the subject — every
musical decision in the piece was made by ear.

The talk follows one material end to end. A density buildup, algorithmically
generated, was correct and unmusical; played in by hand at a MIDI keyboard it
was musical and unplayable, its apex demanding 54.5 attacks per second from an
ensemble capable of 22. The resolution was not a better algorithm but a split:
keep the algorithmic meta-shape, replace the grain source with live human
performance, then compute the nearest playable neighbour — 134 unplayable
collisions to zero. Xenakis, with the interior performed rather than
distributed.

I close with an unplanned result. The frame, adopted to solve my own
compositional problem, migrated to the performer's: the finished score supplies
executable tools rather than prescribing a result — the same reframing, one
domain over.

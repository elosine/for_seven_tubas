# THE ASSEMBLY METHOD — day 21 process record

> How the trance continuation (tranceA003b → f) got built in one sitting:
> pitch sets generated by rule, streams that each advance one item per
> insertion, a live performance whose *timings* became score material, and a
> permutation layer that lets any chunk be swapped without disturbing the
> rest. Written for the paper: the sequence and the reversals, not the code.
> Raw trail: RUNNING_LOG day 21. Sibling record: PHASE_COMPLEXITY.md (the
> theory arc that produced the phase material this method consumes).

---

## 1. The pitch reservoirs: rows grown by rule

The composer's rule, dictated: start from G♯–A♯–B (the pitch classes of the
three multitempo sections already in the piece), then repeatedly **jump up a
fifth** (repeats allowed, not counted) and **come down to one of the pitches
not yet used**, until all twelve are consumed. Octaves randomized inside the
instrument's sounding window.

Eight rows were generated (`scores/row-fifths-01.json`): row 1 from the
G♯–A♯–B seed, rows 2–8 from random starting pitches. Each row is ~13–16
notes, audible as a line in Tuba 1, and doubles as a **reservoir**: later,
each phase-shifting insertion takes the *next* pitch of row 7 as its pitch
world. The row is not a theme — it is an ordered supply of tonal centers.

*(Process note kept honest: the first generation produced seven near-identical
rows — correlated random seeds — caught by reading the output, not by ear.)*

## 2. The chord stream: dictated probabilities, tuned in passes

Material: the piece's own **base chord** (the fifths stack G1–A2–E3–B3–F4,
extracted from the trance section's ground) plus six **species** — the
played-in VERT01 verticals (16, 3, 28, 12, 18, 27), original pitches, no
octave doublings, notes outside the staccato window dropped (or delivered by
cuivre, §4).

Thirty patterns of chord-pulses at 150 BPM, with the *chord re-drawn on every
beat* (base 40% / species 60%). The pattern-length scheme took three spoken
iterations to land — 60/40 over 2–8, then 65/35 over 3–8, then the composer
rejected dice altogether for the shape: *"I want one long and then anywhere
between one or two short — not short-long-short-long."* The final rule is
**structural, not statistical**: groups of one long (6–10 beats) plus one or
two shorts (3–5), randomness only inside the group. A dictated preference
became a grammar.

## 3. The phase stream: a performance becomes the form

From the phase-complexity work (see PHASE_COMPLEXITY.md): the computed
12-rung ladder → the composer's own re-ordering of rungs (11-7-2-3-10-4-6,
an alternating high-low complexity path) → a tempo ramp (87→120, later
87→120 re-spanned by ear) → and then the decisive move: **the composer
performed the sequence on the live rig, and the stopwatch log's step
durations (5.4 / 4.9 / 5.2 / 7.1 / 7.9 / 10.3 s) became the score's phase
chunks.** Play-in capture operating at the level of FORM — when to move —
rather than notes.

Each phase insertion is one step of that performance, pitched on the next
pitch of row 7, as octaves scrambled per player (each player: one steady
tempo, one pitch class, octave changing every attack — a texture whose only
notation problem is entry timing).

## 4. Assembly as alternation

The composer's model, arrived at by correction (the first build wrongly
inserted everything at once): **streams that each advance one item per
insertion.** Phase steps 1, 2, 3…; chord patterns P9, P10, P11…; multitempo
models chosen from the audition battery's unused pool. Every insertion opens
on the next beat of the piece's 0.4 s grid. The running order is literally a
one-line plan:

    phase:1:11.88:fifths, chord:9, phase:2:8.33, chord:10, mt:B,
    chord:11, phase:3, chord:12:1:4:2+4, phase:4

Standing rules accreted during the sitting, each dictated and then made
structural: chords **re-voice onto fresh random parts every beat** (which
also halved the sample-overlap load — a playability gain the composer didn't
ask for); cuivre versions of a blast take the bank's own cuivre notes and are
**written short** (0.2 s, the composer's existing convention — the measured
1.3 s sample ring is a ceiling, not a notation); multitempo material is
lifted **verbatim** from the audition file, extendable past its recorded
length because the lattice is pure math.

## 5. Fluid adjustment: the permutation layer

The composer's requirement, stated as a workflow need: *"changing the pitch
set of a chunk, changing the duration of a chunk… two-three instead of
four."* What makes it facile:

- **Per-chunk dice.** Every chunk's randomness derives from its own plan
  token, so editing chunk 3 cannot re-deal chunk 5. Proven by experiment:
  a mid-plan edit left all eight other chunks identical to 0.000 ms.
- **Automatic reflow.** Chunks lay end to end on the beat grid; lengthening
  or shrinking one moves everything downstream, content untouched.
- **Variant FAMILIES instead of parameter talk.** "PS1 in fifths vs cluster
  vs species 27" became five complete score files identical everywhere but
  the one chunk — the composer compares *hearings*, not settings. Then the
  chosen variant (fifths) became the base for the next family (PS2's five
  pitch worlds, including any pitch set addressable from the audition
  battery by segment number).

The working grain: the composer speaks a change in one sentence; the change
is one token; the re-render is two seconds; versions are letters
(003b → 003c → … → 003f) and families are suffixes.

## 6. What this is, compositionally

The score's continuation is not a document being edited but **a plan being
grown** — banked material (rows, species, performed timings, audition
segments) + deterministic machinery + a one-line running order. Identity
lives in the plan; the files are renderings. The composer's interventions
are of three kinds, all preserved verbatim in the trail: **rules** ("reshuffle
every beat", "one long then one or two short"), **selections** (which rungs,
which models, which segments), and **performances** (the stopwatch run that
became the phase durations). The machinery's job is to make each kind
one sentence long.
